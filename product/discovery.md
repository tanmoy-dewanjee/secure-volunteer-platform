# Discovery questions

Use with the client. If the team already has an answer, confirm it politely rather than re-asking from scratch.

## Project

1. What is the primary objective of this project?
2. What specific problem or challenge are we trying to solve?
3. What are the main goals and desired outcomes?
4. Who are the primary stakeholders, and what are their roles?
5. What assumptions are being made about the project?
6. Who are the end users of this project?
7. What are their key needs, preferences, and pain points?
8. How will this project impact end users?
9. Which features or functionalities matter most to them?
10. How will we validate that the solution meets user needs?
11. How will accessibility be addressed?
12. What existing IT infrastructure must this project integrate with?
13. Are there technical constraints or limitations to consider?
14. What programming languages, frameworks, or platforms must be used?
15. What are the project's data requirements (collection, storage, analysis)?
16. Are there performance or scalability requirements?
17. How often should progress updates be shared with stakeholders?
18. Which communication channels (email, MS Teams) will be used with stakeholders?
19. What reporting format do stakeholders prefer (dashboards, documents, slides)?

## Budget (indirect)

Do not ask the client to fund or approve a real budget. These questions help form a realistic estimate:

1. What is the allocated budget for this project?
2. Are there budgetary constraints or cost considerations?
3. What resources (people, equipment, funding) are already available?
4. Do we need external vendors or contractors?

## Method and tools

1. Does the client have a preferred project management method?
2. Does the client follow a preferred project management tool?
3. Does the client have a current risk management policy?
4. Does the client follow a particular risk matrix?
